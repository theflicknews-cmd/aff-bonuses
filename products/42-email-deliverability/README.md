## How inbox placement actually works

Mailbox providers (Gmail, Outlook, etc.) decide inbox vs. spam based on:
1. **Authentication** — are you really who you say you are? (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)
2. **Reputation** — your sending domain/IP history.
3. **Engagement** — do people open, click, and reply? (or delete/mark spam?)
4. **Content** — spammy words, too many links, bad formatting.
5. **List hygiene** — are you emailing dead/invalid addresses?

You can't control the algorithm, but you control all five inputs.

## The 5 levers you control

**1. Authentication (set once)**
Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for your sending domain. This is the single biggest
technical factor. Most autoresponders provide step-by-step instructions.

**2. Reputation (build over time)**
- Warm up new domains/sending slowly.
- Be consistent (sudden blasts hurt).
- Use a custom domain, not a free address, as the "from."

**3. Engagement (the modern #1 factor)**
- Email people who want to hear from you (good lead magnet = good list).
- Send valuable content, not just pitches.
- Remove/segment people who never open.
- Ask for replies early (boosts reputation).

**4. Content**
- Avoid spam-trigger words and ALL CAPS / excessive punctuation!!!
- Keep a healthy text-to-link ratio (1-2 links often beats 10).
- Don't attach files or use deceptive subject lines.
- Include a clear unsubscribe link.

**5. List hygiene**
- Use double opt-in where possible.
- Clean inactive subscribers regularly (re-engage, then remove).
- Never buy lists (instant reputation death).

## Lifting open rates (so engagement stays high)

- Write subject lines for curiosity/benefit (Product 17).
- Send when your audience is active (test times).
- Keep a consistent, recognizable "from" name.
- Re-engage or prune non-openers (a smaller engaged list beats a big dead one).

## Warning signs you have a deliverability problem

- 🚩 Open rates suddenly drop.
- 🚩 Clicks fall off a cliff.
- 🚩 Subscribers say they can't find your emails.
- 🚩 Spam complaints rise.

## How to use (for the affiliate)

1. Set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC for your sending domain (one-time).
2. Run the per-send checklist before every broadcast.
3. Prioritize engagement: value first, prune non-openers.
4. Monitor open/click trends; act fast if they dip.
